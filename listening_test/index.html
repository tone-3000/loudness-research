NAM loudness metadata — listening test

Real DI clips rendered through a representative set of public TONE3000 models, referred to by anonymised ids (every gear type, guitar and bass; amp-only models go through a cabinet IR at unity in-band gain, as the pedal and web engines do). Pick a normalisation scheme and switch models while the clip plays — exactly what a player does when stepping through presets. The clip position carries over between models, so what you hear is the volume jump between presets, nothing else.

Gains are the engines' arithmetic, −18 − loudness, with the shipped ±12 dB clamp for the current value and the recommended ±18 dB clamp for the proposed one. All renders share one fixed trim so the three schemes are directly comparable; turn your monitor up — the files are deliberately quiet so that no gain ever clips.

Metadata values: current = flat RMS of 1 s pink noise at −15 dBFS; proposed = K-weighted mean-of-dB over a 7-burst −39…−15 dBFS instrument-pink sweep, +3 dB calibration. “What you hear” is the measured K-weighted level of each render plus the gain the scheme applies; the spread of those markers is the thing the metadata is supposed to make small.
